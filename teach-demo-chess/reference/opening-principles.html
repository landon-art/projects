Opening Principles Cheat Sheet

The first ten moves have three jobs. Everything else is detail.

The three jobs

JobWhat it meansTypical moves
1. CentrePut a pawn on, or aim pieces at, e4 d4 e5 d5.e4 d4 as White; …e5 …d5 as Black
2. DevelopKnights and bishops off the back rank, toward the centre. Each piece moves once.Nf3 Nc3 Bc4 Be2
3. King safetyCastle, usually by move 6 to 8. Then connect the rooks.O-O

Source: NM Dane Mattson, Chess Opening Principles (Chess.com).

Rules of thumb

  • Two or three pawn moves, then pieces. Too many pawn moves is the number one beginner opening mistake (FM Luis Fernandez Siles).
  • Knights before bishops. Knights have one good square each; bishops can wait to see where they belong.
  • Don't move the same piece twice before the others are out, unless forced.
  • Queen stays home until minor pieces are developed. An early queen gets chased and loses time.
  • Castle before you attack. A king in the centre is the target of every early attack.
  • Every move, first ask: what did their last move threaten? Checks, captures, threats.

Move-10 self-audit

Stop at move 10 in your game and tick:

CheckYes / No
King castled
Both knights developed
At least one bishop developed
At least one centre pawn on e4/d4 (or e5/d5)
Queen still on her starting square or on a safe square
No piece undefended and attacked

Five or six ticks: you have survived the opening. Fewer: find the move where a tick was lost. That move is the lesson.

A model opening (White)

1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Bc5 4.O-O Nf6 5.d3 d6 6.c3 O-O

Italian Game after 6 moves each. Both sides did all three jobs. This is what "surviving the opening" looks like.
